## Notes

- **`brugada-1.png` filename correction:** the originally requested download URL for this file omitted `(CardioNetworks ECGpedia)` from the actual Commons title and 404'd (a 41KB HTML error page, not an image). Retried with the correct full title from earlier research and it succeeded — table above reflects the correct source.
- **2026-09-17 clinical-content correction (release safety pass):** verified all 4 previously "pending" cases against source Commons/ECGpedia descriptions and removed 3 images whose source metadata contradicted the app's stated diagnosis:
  - `vt-monomorphic-2.jpg` (Vtach, CardioNetworks) — source caption read "Ventricular tachycardia **or idioventricular rhythm**" — not a confirmed monomorphic-VT diagnosis. Removed.
  - `vt-monomorphic-3.jpg` (ECG000032, CardioNetworks) — source page had no description and was Commons-categorized as **"Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia"** — directly contradicts a "Monomorphic VT" case. Removed.
  - `afib-rvr-3.jpg` (DVA2200) — source confirmed "Atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular rate response **and left bundle branch block**," which contradicts the case's "Narrow QRS — no aberrancy" finding. Removed.
  - The remaining `vt-monomorphic-1.jpg` (E000790) source explicitly confirms monomorphic VT (RBBB pattern, monomorphic R in V1, R/S <1 in V6) at a documented rate of **270/min** — the in-app case now reflects that rate instead of the previous unsourced ~160 bpm.
  - `stemi-lateral-1.jpg` (E268): source confirms "lateral wall myocardial infarction" with rightward axis, Q waves in I/aVL, poor anterior R-wave progression, and slight ST elevation in I/aVL with lateral T-wave inversion. It does **not** mention V5-V6 ST elevation or reciprocal III/aVF depression as the in-app case currently states — diagnosis category is correct, but those two specific claimed findings remain unconfirmed by source. Corrected below (see the 2026-09-17 stemi-lateral finding resolution note).
- **2026-09-17 Wellens subtype resolution:** independently checked whether `wellens-1.jpg` (Wellens1.JPG), `wellens-2.jpg` (WellensPainfree.JPG), and `wellens-3.jpg` (Wellens2.JPG) support the app's "Type B" claim, beyond the bare Commons captions checked previously:
  - `WellensPainfree.JPG` (`wellens-2.jpg`) is used on the English Wikipedia "Wellens' syndrome" article (same author, James Heilman, MD) with the caption *"EKG/ECG of the same person when pain-free, note the biphasic T waves in leads V2 and V3."* This directly confirms biphasic T waves in V2-V3 for this specific image.
  - Per LITFL (Life in the Fast Lane, ECG Library — an established clinical ECG reference), the classic de Zwaan classification is: **Type A = biphasic T waves (~25% of cases)**; **Type B = deeply and symmetrically inverted T waves (~75% of cases)**. A confirmed biphasic pattern is therefore consistent with **Type A**, not Type B — the app's prior "Type B" label was not just unconfirmed but the *opposite* of what the one independently-verifiable image actually shows.
  - `Wellens1.JPG` (`wellens-1.jpg`) and `Wellens2.JPG` (`wellens-3.jpg`) are not used in any Wikipedia article or other Commons page ("There are no pages that use this file" on both) — no independent caption or morphology confirmation exists for either beyond the bare "Wellens syndrome" label on their own file pages.
  - **Resolution:** per the safest evidence-based option, removed `wellens-1.jpg` and `wellens-3.jpg` (no independent morphology confirmation) and the "Type B" subtype claim throughout the app (title, answer option, explanation text). Kept only `wellens-2.jpg`, whose biphasic-T-wave-V2-V3 finding is independently confirmed, taught simply as "Wellens syndrome" with no subtype asserted. Did not relabel as "Type A" — that would introduce a new claim beyond what this task authorized; the evidence is noted here for a future decision if wanted.
- **2026-09-17 stemi-lateral finding resolution:** re-checked `stemi-lateral-1.jpg` (E268) for independent usage/confirmation — the file page confirms "There are no pages that use this file" (no Wikipedia article or other Commons page references it), so no source beyond its own Commons description exists. That description states only: sinus rhythm; rightward axis; wide Q waves in I, aVL; poor R-wave progression across the anterior chest leads; slight ST elevation in I, aVL; T-wave inversion in the lateral leads. It does not mention V5-V6 or any precordial lead beyond the anterior R-wave-progression comment, and does not mention leads III or aVF at all. **Resolution:** removed the unsupported "V5-V6 elevation" and "reciprocal III/aVF depression" findings from the app; the case's findings and explanation now state only what the source text directly documents. Diagnosis label kept as "Lateral STEMI" (both "Lateral STEMI" and "lateral myocardial infarction" were pre-authorized labels for this fix; the source's own wording is "lateral wall myocardial infarction").
- **2026-09-17 stemi-lateral vignette timeline correction:** the source text states the patient "had had a myocardial infarction a few months before," associated with a resuscitated VF arrest at that time — i.e. this ECG documents an evolved/established MI (pathologic Q waves, only slight ST elevation, T-wave inversion), not a brand-new event. The in-app vignette previously read "Ongoing chest pain 90 minutes," implying a hyperacute presentation inconsistent with a Q-wave/T-inversion pattern (Q waves do not typically form within 90 minutes of symptom onset). **Resolution:** updated the case `description` to reflect a prior-MI/follow-up framing consistent with the source timeline. Findings, explanation, image mapping, and license/attribution were already correct and untouched.
